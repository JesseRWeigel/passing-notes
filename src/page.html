    What is actually going on

    At every turn you pick one of the legal moves, so your choice is worth log2(number of legal moves) bits and nothing more. The code is the whole game written as one number in a mixed radix, where each digit's radix is how many moves were legal at that point.

    • A position with only one legal move costs zero bits. So do passes. Your opponent's browser works those out from the rules rather than being told.
    • Reading digit k requires replaying moves 0 to k-1 and generating the legal moves at that position, so a mangled code cannot decode into an illegal position. It either fails the checksum or turns into some other legal game.
    • That is legality, and it is not honesty. Whoever holds the state can hand back a legal game that rewinds their blunder. The rewind guard above is the answer, and it only works where the browser lets this page remember things.

    Live mode, and why it is not the default

    WebRTC with the signalling done by hand

    WebRTC available here: no. Serverless in this configuration: yes.

    One of you presses Make an offer and sends the blob. The other pastes it, presses Answer, and sends the reply back. The first player pastes that and the two browsers talk directly. After that, moves travel by themselves.
